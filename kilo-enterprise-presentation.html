The challenge

AI adoption is outpacing AI governance.

Three concerns we hear from risk, security, and platform teams in regulated industries.

Shadow AI

When sanctioned tools are harder than workarounds, developers route around policy. Unmanaged API keys and unvetted models reach production codebases.

Unpredictable spend

Per-token billing resists forecasting. A single heavy session can move a monthly budget line, and finance finds out after the invoice.

Audit gaps

Examiners ask who used which model, on what code, with what result. Most AI tooling cannot produce that record.

Governance

Governance and access control

Decide who uses AI, which models they can reach, and what they can do. Enforce it at the gateway, not by policy memo.

SSO enforcement

Domain-based single sign-on with just-in-time provisioning. Magic-link sign-in is disabled for protected domains, so offboarding is immediate.

Role-based access

Owner, member, and billing manager roles. Billing staff manage spend and invoices without consuming a developer seat.

Model restrictions Enterprise

Provider allow lists and model deny lists enforced at the AI gateway. Requests to unapproved models are blocked before they reach a provider.

Per-member daily limits

Daily spend caps per developer, enforced at the API gateway. A runaway session stops at the cap, not at the invoice.

Cost control

Spend visibility and cost control

Every dollar of AI spend is visible in real time, with alerts before small spikes become budget events.

Usage analytics

Spend broken down by product, model, provider, and individual user. Ninety days of history with daily rollups for trend review.

Anomaly alerts

Hourly burst detection against a trailing 7-day baseline. Rolling 24-hour, 7-day, and 30-day thresholds. Alerts name the top five spend drivers.

Shared credit pool

One organization balance with auto top-up controls and expiration tracking. You pre-fund and set the replenishment rules.

Audit

Audit and compliance evidence

Produce the record an examiner asks for: what happened, when, and who did it.

Immutable audit logs

Every administrative action recorded with actor, timestamp, and before/after state. Settings, membership, SSO, and model restrictions.

Security audit reports

Up to 90 days of finding activity, severity changes, remediation outcomes, and SLA tracking with breach evidence.

Privacy redaction

Credentials, tokens, and PII are redacted before audit data is rendered. Reports are safe to share with reviewers.

Integration audit trail

The MCP Gateway logs lifecycle and authorization events for every connected tool, with credentials stripped before requests go upstream.

Security

Security and data protection

Defense in depth for code, credentials, and agent execution.

Encryption at rest

Provider API keys encrypted with AES-256-GCM. Environment variables sealed with per-user AES-256 keys.

Sandbox execution

Agents run in isolated containers with no standing access to production systems or networks.

Verified remediation

Vulnerability fixes are proposed through a sandbox first, and pull request outcomes are verified before anything merges.

Credential isolation

Developer tokens never reach third-party tools. The gateway strips credentials before proxying requests.

AI risk

AI risk management

Govern AI behavior with the same rigor you apply to the rest of the development lifecycle.

Review analytics

A bounded taxonomy of AI review findings with impact classification. Measure review quality over time, by repository and change type.

Council review

Multiple models review critical changes in parallel. No single point of model failure on high-risk merges.

Review memory

The reviewer retains your team's standards across sessions and stops re-raising findings you have already dismissed.

Configurable gates

Set approval thresholds by repository. Human reviewers stay in the loop for the changes that matter most.
